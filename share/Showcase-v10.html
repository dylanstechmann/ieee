The race in 20 seconds

Start on the green flag, run 3 laps around the oval dodging 3 toy-vehicle obstacles, detour to your assigned pit box after lap 2 for a tire exchange, finish the victory lap and park. Score model: 390 robot points + 10 publicity. Under 2:45 earns a 30-point speed bonus.

FIELD FINDING

Obstacle size decides the 75 obstacle points

Using the organizer guide's plush vehicle (≈8.55 × 8.2 in) on the CAD field, a 10 in robot can pass one without contact in only 0–47% of placements — and a full three-obstacle lap is contact-free in just 0–2.5% of random layouts. If the official toy set is that size, a gentle-contact policy beats dodging. Measuring the real vehicles (official list, $35.95) is the highest-value physical test.

02 — The car & the parts

A test mule first — with two vendor-CAD catches before anything was printed

The first build is deliberately a drivetrain mule (a rolling test rig, not the competition chassis): two Pololu 70:1 37D gearmotors with encoders on a printed deck. Before ordering or printing a single part, the whole stack was modeled against Pololu's own STEP files — which caught two errors that a paper design would have missed.

The catch (CAD measurements, not received parts)

Pololu's gearmotor output shaft sits 7 mm off the gearbox center, and the original mule kit's deck, built as drawn, would have left the wheels 7.09 mm off the floor and the casters rocking 1.08 mm. The corrected v10 deck flips the motors shaft-down, adds printed 6.91 mm caster risers, and ships with a true-scale drill template plus STEP/STL regenerated from caliper-checked dimensions. derived/drivetrain-mule-v10/

03 — Physics

What the drivetrain model says the car can actually do

A motor-physics model (torque-speed curve, grip limit, battery sag, 0.42 ms physics substeps, corrected against hand calculations) replaces guessed speed limits. Agent-run sim Placeholder friction 0.15–0.35 Not measurements

FINDING 01

The outer wheel is the speed limit

1.53×

On a 7.5 in U-turn the outer wheel runs 1.53× the robot's center speed. Model top speed is 21.0–24.3 in/s, but keeping wheel headroom caps the cruise at 12 in/s (outer wheel 18.4 in/s, 88% of the lowest top speed). 16 in/s would need 24.5 in/s at the wheel — undrivable through the U-turns.

FINDING 02

Grip, not the motors, limits accel

0.27 N·m

Each wheel can transmit ≈ 0.27 N·m before slipping (μ 0.35, 5 kg) against the motor's 2.65 N·m extrapolated stall. The 70:1 motors are fine; floor friction is the number the design most needs, and the E03 floor trials are set up to measure exactly that.

FINDING 03

Plan for peak current

6 A+

Drive energy is tiny (0.04–0.05 Wh per mission) and cruise is ≈ 0.31 A, but full-command spins hit the assumed 3 A per-motor limit. Size the fuse and battery wiring for 6 A plus electronics; battery choice is set by peak current, sag and swap speed — not capacity.

The tightest moment in every run is a square base spinning in the pit box: 0.27–0.61 in from the wall.

Also in the loop: localization & the fallback

In simulation, cheap low-range sensors plus gyro and encoders keep the pose within ≈ 1 in of truth through a whole mission; a reflectance-only fallback also finishes, which matters if the camera struggles with glare on real paint. Glare over 5–10% of the AprilTag (tagStandard41h12, IDs 1–3) broke single-frame decoding in tests, so the detector votes over several frames.

05 — Honest limits

What this is not

  • Nothing is physical. No parts ordered, no field built, no bench or floor trials, no robot. The mule deck is drawn, not printed.
  • Parameters are placeholders. Friction (0.15–0.35), mass, inertia and battery resistance await E02 bench and E03 floor measurements.
  • Camera, flag and lap line are not modeled. Tag and flag events are simply given to the software.
  • Vehicles are ghosts. A touched obstacle neither resists nor moves, so contact behavior is unknown until real-set trials.
  • No trained policy. The RL interface exists but no learning is planned before real sensor data (and none is claimed).
  • Field geometry is nominal CAD (June 2026, pre-V3) and the built field may vary ±1 in per element.
  • Agent-run ≠ qualified. Simulations are leads to check, not measurements; earlier overclaims (e.g. "20/20 episodes") were corrected in ERRATA-2026-11.md.
  • Rule questions are open. Ten are drafted for the official Discord Q&A; none is posted, and official answers can change legal routes.

06 — Next

From digital to physical: the next four moves

Everything above exists to de-risk the first purchases and the first build weekend. The gate plan targets a concept design review by Dec 13, 2026, a fabrication release Jan 10, and a full practice field well before the Feb reliability campaign.

MOVE 01

Post the rule questions

Ten Q&A items are drafted with manual references (envelope & round base, tag location, obstacle vehicles and placement, track-marking legality, pit access, lap counting, exchange & parking, start flag, field tolerances, publicity). The official Discord Q&A outranks the manuals — answers reshape the concept.

MOVE 02

Order A: the drivetrain

$177.25 researched (motors, brackets, hubs, wheels, casters). On arrival: caliper-check against the inspection templates, then print the corrected mule deck. This is the first physical evidence in the whole project.

MOVE 03

Build the practice field

Materials from the organizer's own list ($182.39 including paint, start flag and the official obstacle set; brushes/tape under $35). Build it, then measure it as built — the ±1 in tolerance is a designed-in variation the robot must survive.

MOVE 04

Measure, then re-baseline

Bench characterization (E02) and floor trials (E03) replace the placeholder friction and motor values; tire-replica pickup tests pick the exchange mechanism. Every measurement flows back into the simulator and the CAD.
