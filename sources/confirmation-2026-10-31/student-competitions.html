Registration

There are 2 registrations that students need to be aware of! Conference Registration and Competition Registration. 

Conference Registration: Every student competing in a student competition must be registered for SoutheastCon 2027 by the start of the student competitions. There is a cost for registering for the conference and registration and costs are addressed on the SoutheastCon main page. Students must be an IEEE member to compete in student competitions and the conference registration will ask for your IEEE member number. Therefore, you will want to be registered as an IEEE member before you register for the conference in order to get the IEEE member conference rate. 

Competition Registration: Each competition has its own registration process and is detailed on the tabs below. There is no cost to register for a competition. Students can register for the competitions before they register for the conference as long as they make sure they are registered for the conference before the start of the competition. 

Hardware Competition

Hardware Competition

 
The 2027 IEEE SoutheastCon Hardware Competition game is Stock Car Race. Teams design, build, test, and program a fully autonomous robot to complete the game tasks defined in the official competition manuals. The Hardware Competition is part of IEEE Region 3 SoutheastCon and is designed to provide a hands-on engineering and robotics experience for student teams.

Competition Overview

Stock Car Race is an autonomous robotics time-trial challenge. Each robot begins in the start box, enters the racecourse, completes three laps, makes a required pit stop after lap one or lap two, and finishes the course in three minutes or less. The game also includes three randomly placed vehicle obstacles, assigned pit stop areas, a tire change task, and additional scoring opportunities for clean laps, finishing the course in less than 2 minutes and 45 seconds, and raising a school flag during the final lap.

Teams should review both Game Manual 1 and Game Manual 2 carefully. Game Manual 1 covers the overall competition structure, team eligibility, robot rules, inspection, sequestering, accommodations, and judging. Game Manual 2 covers the Stock Car Race game, field setup, gameplay rules, scoring, AprilTags, bill of materials, spare tire details, obstacle placement, and the pit stop challenge.

Team Eligibility and Participation

Two types of teams are described in the current rules: Main Teams and Open Teams. Main Teams come from universities in IEEE Region 3, with one Main Team allowed per university. All members of a Main Team must be undergraduate students at the university the team represents. Open Teams have no limits or restrictions on where members can come from.

Both Main Teams and Open Teams must register to compete. Additional registration information will be posted on the SoutheastCon website and announced through the official SoutheastCon Discord.

Software Competition

Software Competition

 
The IEEE Region 3 SoutheastCon 2027 Software Competition is designed to engage students in software-focused disciplines through creativity and open-ended problem solving. Teams simulate producing a software product for a fictitious customer. The customer provides a single set of requirements defining the product functionality, and each team delivers a product that meets those requirements.

Competition Overview

The competition is conducted in three parts: a Critical Design Review (CDR), a Business Presentation, and a Product Demonstration and Test.

  • Critical Design Review: A presentation by the team to a panel of judges describing the product design and justifying the choices made, followed by a brief question and answer session. CDR presentations are expected to last 10 to 15 minutes, and a firm time limit will be provided at the time of the competition. The CDR is not intended to be permission to proceed with the design, so teams should begin product development before the CDR.
  • Business Presentation: A slide presentation judged by judges from the business community, based on the completeness and professionalism of the presentation and the presenter.
  • Product Demonstration and Test: A technical panel of judges reviews the code and runs test cases to determine how well the product meets the customer’s requirements.

Each team provides three deliverables: a business slide presentation describing the product design, delivered at final judging on the team’s own device; the final software product source code, delivered before the end of the competition; and the final executable software product, available on the team’s device at the end of the competition for the judges to review and run test cases. Executable deliverables are submitted via GitHub. If a team does not produce a working product, it may deliver the source code along with documentation describing the product design and a list of the requirements the product is intended to meet.

All teams are judged using the following rubric:

CDR: 50 points

  • Completeness of design description: 25
  • How well design meets requirements: 15
  • Answering questions: 10

Business Presentation: 25 points

  • Completeness of Presentation: 10
  • Slide quality/Professionalness: 10
  • Professionalness of Presenter: 5

Demonstration: 25 points

  • Percentage of requirements met (via test cases): 8
  • User experience – ease of use of product: 7
  • Code Review – quality and SW Eng best practices: 10

Total: 100 points

Team Composition and Eligibility

The competition is divided into the Main Competition and the Open Competition. Any IEEE Region 3 Student Branch may field one Main Competition team. To encourage broader participation, additional teams from the same student branch and teams from outside Region 3 may participate in the Open Competition. All teams are judged using the same criteria.

Teams may consist of a maximum of four undergraduate students, one graduate and two undergraduate students, or two graduate students. In the Main Competition, all team members must be members of the same IEEE Student Branch.

All team members must be physically present at IEEE Region 3 SoutheastCon 2027 from noon on the first day of the competition until the conclusion of competition judging. Teams will not be sequestered during the competition.

Important Notes

Code written by team members prior to the competition is not permitted in either hard or soft copy form. Teams may bring textbooks, notes, and any documentation they feel might be helpful, and must bring laptops for product development along with any power and communications accessories required for laptop use.

Each team must provide a SchoolNameCREDITS.txt file listing all required attributions, even if no attributions apply. Standard programming libraries and APIs, development tools, and software are permitted without attribution. AI-generated content (the tool used must be disclosed), third-party code libraries, and public domain or Creative Commons-licensed content are permitted with attribution. Private, unlicensed, or copyrighted content or code, content or code obtained directly from an individual not on the team, and plagiarism are not permitted. Violation of the attribution rules will result in disqualification.

The rules document includes two example products, a Trip Planner and a Financial Planner, of similar complexity and style to the competition product to help teams prepare.

Teams may schedule time to ask the organizers about the requirements, and the questions and answers will be published on Discord. The Discord server will be monitored to answer questions about the competition. Join the Software Competition channel at https://discord.gg/dfr36XM556.

Circuits Competition

January 1, 2027 – Registration will open. Teams can register for the competition here.

March 1, 2027 – Registration for the competition closes.

April 16, 2027 – Competition onsite at SoutheastCon.

The competition will be a four-hour, hands-on circuit design and embedded programming challenge. Teams will have 30 minutes before the competition to set up, four hours to design and build their solution, and 30 minutes afterward to clean up and leave the competition room.

  • Setup: 1:30–2:00 PM
  • Competition: 2:00–6:00 PM
  • Cleanup and exit: 6:00–6:30 PM

Team Size and Composition

Each IEEE R3 student branch may enter one main team and may also register additional optional open teams, and non-R3 teams can register one open team. Because of space and resource limitations, not all open teams may be accepted. The status of open teams will be confirmed within one week after registration closes, based on available space and resources.

Teams may have up to five undergraduate-equivalent students. Each graduate student counts as two undergraduate students for purposes of the team-size limit. Permitted combinations include:

  • 5 undergraduates
  • 1 graduate student + 3 undergraduates
  • 2 graduate students + 1 undergraduate

Teams are encouraged to have a mix of technical and communication skills. Useful experience may include:

  • Embedded software development
  • Arduino and ESP32 programming
  • Digital and analog circuit design
  • Schematic capture and documentation
  • Hardware troubleshooting and prototyping
  • Technical writing and proposal/report development
  • Photo and video documentation
  • AI-assisted tools and prompting

What to Expect

The IEEE Region 3 Circuit Design Competition will provide each participating team with all components, equipment, and materials needed to complete the challenge. Teams must provide their own laptops. Teams may bring their own tools and test equipment, such as oscilloscopes, multimeters, pliers, and similar equipment, but may not bring their own electronic components, Arduino or ESP32 boards, USB cables, breadboards, or other competition hardware.

The specific competition challenge and its objectives will not be released in advance. Teams will receive the challenge when the competition begins at 2:00 PM. Teams will then have four hours to design, build, test, and demonstrate a proof-of-concept prototype. In addition to the primary challenge, teams may have opportunities to complete optional tasks for additional credit within the time available.

Teams must document their work through a written report and video submission within the four-hour competition time limit. Submission requirements and deadlines will be provided with the competition challenge.

The challenge is designed to be accessible to students who have completed approximately one year of EE/ECE coursework or equivalent hands-on experience. Teams should expect to work with a combination of analog and digital electronics, embedded software, and microcontroller-based systems, including the Arduino Mega 2560 and ESP32.

The competition is designed to reward not only a working prototype, but also engineering design, problem solving, implementation, documentation, and the ability to work effectively under a strict time limit.
